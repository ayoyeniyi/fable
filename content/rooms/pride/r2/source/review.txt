round 1:
The room has the right furniture, but it is drenched in a uniform orange cast with no candlelit falloff, and the walls behind and beside the reader are large and blank. I've cooled and lowered the overhead fill so the candles, fire and moon make pools of light, given the walls upper panelling, and dressed the south wall with a pier glass, candelabrum and chairs. The reader's position, the loo table and the reading direction stay as they were.

CRITIQUE:
- All views: the room is washed in a uniform saturated orange-amber, flat and evenly bright, not a hushed late-evening room lit by candles and fire with darker corners.
- View 4: the wall behind the reader is almost empty, just one picture over a band of dado; it reads as unfinished.
- Views 2 and 3: the large plain upper walls above the dado (the south and east returns) have no structure.
- Views 1 and 4: the picture canvases are flat brown voids that read as empty frames; there is too little tonal difference from the frames.
- View 5: the floor is a broad featureless brown expanse and the rug is undersized for a drawing-room this large.
- View 1: the passage seen through the open door is flat and lit like the room, without the dim, deeper tone of a corridor.

round 2:
The pictures read as empty frames, the room is washed in a uniform amber, and the heavy black upper-panel outlines make every wall busy. The revision pulls the pictures off the wall face, drops the upper panels, cools and lowers the candle fill, and gives the floor, rug and mahogany distinct, darker tones. It keeps the reader, the reading direction and the layout, and adds a few lived-in touches.

CRITIQUE:
- Views 1, 3 and 4: almost every picture frame shows bare wall or flat brown inside. The canvases sit at the wall face, behind the upper panel mouldings, so the room seems hung with empty frames.
- All views: the whole room is soaked in one orange-amber cast. Pale painted walls read mustard, and the floor, rug and mahogany merge into one orange-brown, so it feels flat and over-lit rather than candlelit at night.
- Views 1, 3 and 4: the tall upper panels draw heavy black outlines round every wall bay. This is busy and cartoonish, and turning round (view 4) fills the eye with them.
- View 5: the rug is a plain orange slab, barely distinguished from the floor, with no pattern or contrast.
- Views 1 and 3: the furniture is all one orange mahogany, with nothing on the sofa and little out of line beyond the card chairs; the room feels staged rather than just left.
