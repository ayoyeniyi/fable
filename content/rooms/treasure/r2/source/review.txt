round 1:
The worst problems are that the room reads as an evenly lit, saturated orange daytime room rather than a hushed firelit night, and that the bookcases stop well short of "floor to high ceiling", leaving bare plaster walls.

CRITIQUE:
- All views: light is flat and orange from wall to wall, and the window panes in View 2 glow daylight blue. Nothing reads as a dark winter night lit by a fire, candles and moonlight; the overhead fill lamp and the warm ochre plaster flatten everything.
- View 4: behind the reader is a large bare wall with one picture and two cases at the edges. Views 1 and 2 show about 1.4 m of empty plaster above the 2.8 m cases. The room is not "lined with bookcases" floor to high ceiling.
- View 5: the main rug is one huge, oversaturated, featureless orange plane filling the downward view.
- View 2: the library steps read as two stacked plain crates beside the bookcase.
- View 3: the fireplace barely glows and does not light the hearth area, so the room has no warm centre.
- View 1: the east and west walls still have gaps near the corners, and there is little sense of the squire's world on the surfaces (no globe or charts).

round 2:
The library reads as a library, but the bookcases stop far short of the high ceiling and leave bare wall everywhere; the light is a flat amber wash rather than a firelit night, and a black slab of rug sits at the reader's feet. I've run taller cases along every wall with more busts and trimmed the over-bright fill so the fire and candles carry the room. I also removed the dark rug field and cooled the walls and ceiling so moon and fire separate.

CRITIQUE:
- Views 1–4: the bookcases are short (2.9 m under a 4.2 m ceiling) and spaced apart, so wide bands of bare yellow wall show. This is "bookcases line every wall from floor to ceiling" done halfway, worst on the empty south and east walls.
- All views: the lighting is an even, saturated amber wash with no dark corners or pools of light. It reads as a lit stage set, not a hushed firelit winter night with cool moonlight at the windows.
- View 5: the inner rug renders as a near-black blurred slab at the reader's feet, and the main rug is almost the same colour as the floor.
- View 4: a single bookcase stands alone in the middle of a bare panelled wall behind the reader, with two empty frames.
- Views 1 and 3: the busts are sparse, and the two marble pedestals beside the door read as stubby posts rather than library furniture.
- Views 1–3: the plaster and ceiling are warm and pale, which pushes the whole room toward yellow.
