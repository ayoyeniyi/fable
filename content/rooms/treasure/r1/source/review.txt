round 1:
The worst faults are the faceted cliffs with slab-like turf caps and the bare, plaster-smooth beach. I rebuilt the cliffs as overlapping, finer, frost-dulled rock masses with talus at their feet, and broke up the beach with clustered shingle and frosted tufts. I also pulled the big rock back to the turn of the beach, made the sea more silvery, and kept the reader, the sun and the inn where they were.

CRITIQUE:
- Views 1–3: the cliffs and headland are coarse faceted icosphere lumps, and their turf caps sit on top like separate green slabs, so nothing reads as layered rock.
- Views 1 and 5: the beach right under the reader is one smooth, pale, plaster-like plane with no shingle texture, pebbles or frost detail.
- View 1: the big rock fills the near left edge of the reading view instead of standing in the middle distance at the turn of the beach.
- View 2: the turf caps are a saturated yellow-green that is wrong for a grey hoarfrost morning.
- View 1: the sea is a flat dull blue rather than quiet and silvery, and the bare cliff feet have no fallen rock or talus.
- View 4: the slope up to the inn is a big empty grey field with only a few tiny shrubs.

round 2:
CRITIQUE:
- View 1 and View 2: the cliffs and headland are huge, smooth, faceted blobs like clay plates. They have no strata, ledges or layered rock, so the whole cove reads as a low-poly toy.
- View 1: the sea and the shingle are the same pale blue-grey, so the reading direction is one featureless plain to the horizon, with no shoreline, wrack or silvery water.
- View 5 and View 1: the levelled beach around the reader is bare and smooth, with only two or three pebbles; nothing reads as frosted shingle.
- View 3: the rowing boat is a single thin band with no inside or bottom boards, so it reads as a ring lying on the ground.
- View 4: the cliff ends are sharp blob wedges, and the gorse bushes are dark balls on stalks; both are artificial against the otherwise good inn.
- View 2: the talus and the big rock are lost against the blob faces, so "the big rock at the turn of the beach" does not stand out.
