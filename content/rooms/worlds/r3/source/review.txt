round 1:
The worst problems are that the room is lit evenly, so nothing fades into shadow, that the wall behind the reader is bare, and that the overmantel mirror and mantel ornaments are lost inside the chimney breast. The revision cuts the ceiling fill and lets the pink lamp and candles carry the room, furnishes the south wall, and drops the breast so the glass and clock show. It also deepens the shade, rug and curtain colours.

CRITIQUE:
- View 1: the whole room is evenly and brightly lit, with a near-white ceiling, so nothing "fades into soft shadow" beyond the lamplight; the ceiling fill lamp is far too strong.
- View 4: the south wall behind the reader is a huge bare expanse above the dado, with no furniture or pictures; it reads as unfinished.
- View 2: the overmantel mirror and the mantel clock and vases are missing, buried inside the chimney breast, which leaves a tall plain column and a bare mantel shelf.
- View 1: the "pink" lamp shade reads as plain white, so the rosy glow the scene is built around is lost.
- Views 1 and 5: the rug is a flat salmon colour and the curtains are a washed-out pink; both are too light for a lamplit Victorian dining room.
- View 3: the bookcase stands alone on an otherwise plain wall.

round 2:
CRITIQUE:
- View 1: the reader looks straight down the length of the white-clothed table, so the page floats over the brightest, busiest thing in the room (cloth, lamp, glass and food); the reading direction is neither calm nor low in contrast.
- Views 1–4: the whole room is evenly washed in bright rosy-tan light up to the cornice. Nothing "fades into soft shadow" beyond the lamp, so it reads as a lit showroom, not a summer night.
- Views 2–4: the pale wallpaper leaves huge plain upper walls, and with flat light they read unfinished; a deep Victorian red would hold shadow and feel period.
- View 5 and View 1: the tablecloth is a rigid box with vertical slab sides, with no drape or flare at the hem.
- Views 2–4: the picture canvases are flat mid-tones that read as empty card in frames; darker, varnished-looking canvases would read as old oils.
