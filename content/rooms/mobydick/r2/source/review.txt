round 1:
**CRITIQUE:**
- Views 3 and 1, the window and door: the room sits at street level, and the open door looks straight out onto the brick house and street. That breaks "second-floor back room in an inn".
- View 1, the patchwork counterpane: it is oversaturated, toy-like squares, and the loose "triangle" boxes read as scattered books rather than pieced cloth.
- Views 4 and 2, the walls: the wall behind the reader is completely bare, and the fireplace wall is a big blank plaster field. Combined with the plain smooth floor, the room reads as unfinished, not lived in.
- View 5, the papered fireboard: it shows as a blank white panel, and the whale-striking scene is almost invisible.
- Lighting throughout: the walls are near-white and evenly lit by the ceiling fill. It reads as flat studio light, not pale, cold dawn from a narrow street.
- View 5, the floor: it is a uniform brown sheet with no boards. The basin on the chest reads like a cushion.

round 2:
**CRITIQUE:**
- View 4: the peacoat behind the reader is a faceted, pointed cone hanging off the peg rail; it reads as no garment at all and is the ugliest thing in the room.
- Views 2 and 5: the papered fireboard is a blank cream panel. The tilted board has swallowed the whale, boat and harpooner, so the room loses its signature image.
- View 1: the lashed hammock is a rigid smooth tube, and the seaman's bag and the oilskin by the bed head are smooth egg blobs. The sea gear in the reading direction looks like placeholder shapes.
- Views 1, 2 and 3: the walls are large plain plaster planes from skirting to cornice; nothing below the dado and little above it breaks them up.
- View 5: the rug is a big pale featureless slab right at the reader's feet.
- Lighting and the cold dawn colour work, and the bed and counterpane read well; keep them.
